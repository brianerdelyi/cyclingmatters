Why It Matters

Report here.
Because your incident matters.

01
Your record exists
Most dangerous incidents vanish without a trace. A report on CycleReport creates a timestamped, geotagged record captured close to the incident — a contemporaneous account that can support insurance claims, strengthen a police report, or establish a pattern if the same location or vehicle appears again.
02
The data speaks
Police services only publish collision data — the worst outcomes. And most police reporting forms aren't designed to capture anything less: near-misses, harassment, dangerous passes, doorings that don't injure — these incident types have no structured home in a standard police report. CycleReport is built specifically for them. That unreported shadow is where most cyclists actually live, and it's the data that drives real change — with police services, City planners, and infrastructure teams.
03
Accountability, published
A police service can't publish data that reflects on its own gaps. We can. Every incident report is anonymized and added to the public map in real time — hotspot clusters, incident types, affected corridors. Raw, unfiltered cycling safety data that journalists, councillors, and city planners can see, cite, and act on. No waiting for an annual report. No filtering through a press office.
04
A trusted third party
CycleReport is operated by Cycling Matters — an independent cycling advocacy organization, not a government body or police service. That independence is the point. Cyclists trust us. Police services can trust our data. Cities can cite it. When a Toronto cyclist was doored by a police officer in 2026 and cited for not wearing a helmet — which is legal for adults in Ontario — that's exactly why an independent third party matters.
05
Built to work together
CycleReport is designed to complement police services — not replace them. We're ready to connect directly with online reporting systems the moment integration options are available. Our data is structured and waiting.
06
You'll hear back
This shouldn't be radical: every report gets a response. Instant acknowledgement. A quality review process ensures the data stays accurate and credible. Status updates at every stage. A feedback loop that most cyclists have never experienced from a reporting platform.
Beyond policing

Some dangers are built into the road itself.

When incidents cluster at the same intersection month after month, that's not just a driver problem — it's a design problem. CycleReport's public map makes those patterns visible to everyone: high-incident corridors, dangerous intersections, and the roads where cyclists consistently feel unsafe — missing protected lanes, blind corners, poor sightlines, conflict points with turning vehicles.

That data is public and open. City planners, advocacy groups, journalists, councillors, researchers — anyone can see it, cite it, and act on it. Transparency is how patterns become pressure. Your report is part of that public record.
